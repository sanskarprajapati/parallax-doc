## Narration

Read as one take. Years are spelled out so every TTS reads them the same way.

> [serious] Nineteen forty-four. D-Day. The largest amphibious operation in history.
> It's genuinely impressive as a logistical achievement, and the men who died on the
> beaches were brave. That's not in question. What is in question is the myth that
> surrounds it, and the reasons it was launched when it was. Here's the strategic
> reality. By June nineteen forty-four, Germany was already losing the war... in the
> East. Most German soldiers killed in this war died fighting the Soviet Union. For two
> years, Stalin had demanded a second front, and for two years, the Western Allies
> delayed. At Tehran, in late nineteen forty-three, they finally promised one: France,
> by May. Sixteen days after Normandy, the Red Army launched Operation Bagration, and
> shattered an entire German army group. D-Day didn't defeat Hitler. It decided who
> would stand in Europe when he fell.
